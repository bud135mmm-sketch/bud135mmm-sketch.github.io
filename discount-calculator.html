Why stacked discounts aren't just added together

A common mistake during sales season: seeing "25% off" and then an extra "10% off" and assuming that's 35% off the original price. It isn't — the second discount applies to the already-reduced price, not the original, so two stacked discounts are always slightly less generous than their sum suggests. This calculator handles that correctly if you enter a second, stacked discount.

The math

StepFormula
Price after first discountoriginal × (1 − discount₁ ÷ 100)
Price after second discountprevious price × (1 − discount₂ ÷ 100)

For example: a $100 item with 25% off drops to $75. A further 10% off that $75 (not the original $100) brings it to $67.50 — a combined discount of 32.5%, not 35%. The gap grows with bigger percentages, which is worth knowing before assuming a "50% + 20%" sign means 70% off.
