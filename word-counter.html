Why word count matters more than people expect

Word and character limits show up everywhere: a tweet or post with a hard character cap, a college essay with a word minimum, a meta description for SEO capped around 155–160 characters, a resume bullet point that reads better under a certain length, or a text message that splits into multiple parts past 160 characters. Word processors count differently depending on settings (some exclude footnotes, headers, or captions), so a dedicated, transparent counter is often more reliable when a limit actually matters.

How reading time is estimated

This tool estimates reading time using 200 words per minute, a commonly cited average adult silent-reading speed. It's an estimate, not a guarantee — dense technical writing reads slower, and skimmable prose reads faster, but 200 wpm is a reasonable middle-of-the-road figure for general audiences and is the same rate used by most publishing platforms that display a "X min read" estimate.
