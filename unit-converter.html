Why these units still trip people up

Most of the world uses the metric system (kilometers, kilograms, Celsius), while the United States primarily uses imperial units (miles, pounds, Fahrenheit). That split shows up constantly — a recipe from a UK site listing grams, a US road sign in miles, a fitness app logging weight in pounds when your scale reads kilograms. None of the conversions are complicated math, but doing them in your head reliably is harder than it looks, especially with temperature, since Celsius and Fahrenheit don't share a zero point.

The formulas behind each conversion

ConversionFormula
Kilometers → Mileskm × 0.621371
Miles → Kilometersmi × 1.60934
Kilograms → Poundskg × 2.20462
Pounds → Kilogramslb × 0.453592
Celsius → Fahrenheit(°C × 9/5) + 32
Fahrenheit → Celsius(°F − 32) × 5/9

A quick sanity check for temperature: 0°C is 32°F (freezing), and 100°C is 212°F (boiling water at sea level) — useful reference points if you ever need to estimate without a calculator at all.
